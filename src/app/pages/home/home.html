3D weather visualization

Kenobi Project

Kenobi Project is an interactive globe that shows, almost in real time, how the air moves and behaves over the planet. It turns weather data into animated wind currents and color maps that users can rotate, zoom into and explore.

Open the globe

What it shows

The globe displays thousands of particles that follow the direction and speed of the wind in each region, leaving trails that reveal the large circulations of the atmosphere. The particles can be colored by air temperature or by wind speed, as the user chooses.

Color layers

On the surface, the project offers two color layers: temperature and sea-level air pressure. Because both paint the same surface, turning one on turns the other off. The wind layer is independent and can stay on together with either of them. Each layer has its own legend.

Information per point

When you hover over the globe, the project shows a small window with the location's coordinates, temperature, wind speed and direction, pressure and humidity. When you click, it fetches the detailed data for that point, including astronomical information.

Where the data comes from

The data comes from the public Open-Meteo API, which the project queries just once for the whole globe. The query covers a grid of 264 points spaced 15° apart and is refreshed periodically. From these points, the project interpolates a continuous, smooth field that fills the regions between measurements.

How it was built

The project uses Angular, with standalone components and signals, and three.js for 3D rendering. Layer colors are computed on the graphics card, and the wind field calculation is split into small slices so the screen does not freeze. The globe is only redrawn when something changes, which saves processing power and battery.

Designed to be fast

The globe is created in the background while the user is still on this page and stays in memory when changing routes. That is why it appears ready as soon as the project opens, without reloading on every visit.

Next steps

Ideas for future implementations

The project plans to evolve from a weather visualization into a mathematical model that compares the current state of the atmosphere with what is normal for each region of the planet. The model will combine the three air variables, humidity, temperature and pressure, and will use geolocation to associate each point on the globe with its climate zone and its historical average of about 30 years, showing whether the current value is above or below what is expected. It also plans to include surface factors, such as vegetation cover, exposed soil and degree of urbanization, which change the microclimate of each place, based on NASA's open databases. The focus will be the model, not the forecasting of events.
